# Food Bank Aid: Community Fundraising Strategy
## 6-Month Plan — April to September 2026

---

### Assumptions

- The £500,000 annual shortfall is the baseline gap; community fundraising is one strand of a broader income strategy, not the sole solution.
- "Community groups" means sports clubs, local organisations, garden centres, faith groups, schools, and similar bodies with their own membership bases.
- The fundraiser leads relationship management; the marcomms lead supports with materials; the CEO provides senior endorsement and attends key relationship moments.
- Volunteers are available for practical support (collections, events, stewarding) but are not expected to lead donor relationships.
- No new budget is assumed beyond what is already allocated; this strategy works within existing resources.
- Success is defined as: building a sustainable pipeline of community partnerships that generate recurring or growing income contributions.

---

## The Opportunity

Community groups have three things Food Bank Aid needs: networks, goodwill, and the capacity to fundraise on our behalf. A sports club with 200 members, a garden centre with 5,000 seasonal customers, or a faith congregation with 100 regular attendees can each become a meaningful funding channel — if approached well.

The goal is not to ask for a one-off donation. It is to build a relationship where the community group becomes an active advocate and fundraising partner.

---

## Strategic Approach

### Core Model: The Partnership Cycle

Each community relationship follows a three-stage cycle:

1. **Connect** — identify the group, make contact, establish shared interest
2. **Activate** — agree a fundraising activity or campaign that suits the group
3. **Steward** — report impact, celebrate results, deepen the relationship for the next cycle

This cycle is repeatable. A well-stewarded partner will activate again the following year and often increases their contribution.

---

## 6-Month Execution Plan

### Month 1–2: Build the Foundation

**Goal:** Set up the infrastructure and identify the first cohort of target partners.

**Actions:**
- Define an ideal community partner profile (size, geography, mission alignment)
- Research and shortlist 30–40 prospective community groups in the catchment area
- Create a one-page partnership proposition — what Food Bank Aid offers (recognition, impact reporting, volunteer experiences) and what we ask (a fundraising activity, a collection, a sponsored event)
- Prepare a lightweight CRM tracker (spreadsheet or simple tool) to log contacts, conversations, and pipeline stage
- Brief volunteers on how to flag community connections they already have

**Staff responsibilities:**
- Fundraiser: lead on prospecting and tracker setup
- Marcomms: produce partnership proposition and materials
- CEO: endorse the approach and write a personal outreach letter template

---

### Month 3–4: Begin Outreach and First Activations

**Goal:** Contact the shortlisted groups and secure the first 10 active partnerships.

**Actions:**
- Begin personalised outreach to all 30–40 prospects (email + follow-up call)
- Hold introductory meetings with interested groups (target: 15–20 meetings)
- Co-design a fundraising activity with each willing partner — match the format to their group (e.g. sponsored walk for a running club, collection point for a garden centre, quiz night for a social club)
- Activate first fundraising events or campaigns
- Capture volunteer sign-ups from partner group members

**Staff responsibilities:**
- Fundraiser: leads all meetings and negotiations
- Marcomms: creates bespoke materials for each activation where needed
- CEO: attends 2–3 high-potential meetings as senior endorsement

**Volunteer involvement:**
- Use volunteers to support events (stewarding, collection counting, logistics)
- Identify 2–3 volunteer "community ambassadors" who can help represent Food Bank Aid at partner events

---

### Month 5–6: Steward, Report, and Scale

**Goal:** Report back to active partners, celebrate results, and plan the next cycle.

**Actions:**
- Send personalised impact reports to each active partner (how much raised, how it was used)
- Host a small thank-you event or acknowledgement moment (can be virtual or low-cost)
- Use results to qualify the pipeline: identify which partnerships are worth deepening
- Begin outreach to the next cohort of prospective partners (building on learnings)
- Prepare a 6-month review report for trustees with headline numbers and a go/no-go recommendation

**Staff responsibilities:**
- Fundraiser: manages stewardship process and prepares the trustee report
- Marcomms: produces impact reports and thank-you materials
- CEO: presents the 6-month review to trustees

## KPIs and Leading Indicators

These indicators are designed to give the team early warning on whether the strategy is working — before end income figures are confirmed.

### Leading Indicators (check monthly)

| Indicator | Target by Month 3 | Target by Month 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective groups identified | 30+ | 50+ |
| Outreach contacts made | 20+ | 40+ |
| Introductory meetings held | 10+ | 20+ |
| Active partnerships agreed | 5+ | 15+ |
| Fundraising activities launched | 3+ | 12+ |
| New volunteer contacts from partner groups | 10+ | 30+ |

### Outcome Indicators (check monthly)

| Indicator | Target by Month 6 |
|---|---|
| Total income from community partnerships | £25,000–£50,000 |
| Average income per active partnership | £2,000–£4,000 |
| Partnerships that agree to repeat next year | 60%+ |
| New regular donors referred by partners | 20+ |

### Lagging Indicator (assess at 6 months)

| Indicator | Threshold for "go" decision |
|---|---|
| Annualised income run-rate from partnerships | £50,000+ |
| Number of self-sustaining partner relationships | 8+ |
| Volunteer hours contributed via partner groups | 200+ hours |

---

## Decision Framework at 6 Months

At the end of month 6, the fundraiser will present a review to trustees with a recommendation using the following framework:

| Outcome | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Annualised run-rate ≥ £100,000 and 10+ active partnerships | **Scale:** dedicate more resource, target 30+ partnerships in year 2 |
| Annualised run-rate £50,000–£100,000 and 8+ partnerships | **Continue:** sustain current approach, refine targeting and activation |
| Annualised run-rate £25,000–£50,000 and 5+ partnerships | **Review:** assess whether a different partner profile or activation model would improve results |
| Annualised run-rate < £25,000 or fewer than 5 partnerships | **Pivot:** this strand alone is insufficient; consider blending with other fundraising approaches |

## Resource Allocation

### Staff Time (estimated per month)

| Role | Community fundraising time |
|---|---|
| Fundraiser | 60–70% of role |
| Marcomms | 20% of role (materials, impact reports) |
| CEO | 10% of role (key meetings, trustee reporting) |

### Volunteer Time

- Volunteer community ambassadors: 2–3 volunteers, ~4 hours/month each
- Event support volunteers: recruited as needed per activation

### Budget

- Partnership materials (printed one-pagers, thank-you cards): £500–£1,000
- Small thank-you event at month 6: £500–£1,000
- CRM tool (if upgrading from spreadsheet): £0–£600/year

---

## Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fundraiser capacity is stretched | Prioritise the top 20 prospects; use volunteers as ambassadors for lighter-touch outreach |
| Groups are interested but slow to activate | Provide a menu of low-effort options (e.g. collection tin, online giving page) alongside bigger activations |
| Income arrives too late to measure at 6 months | Track commitments and pledged income alongside cash received |
| Volunteer involvement creates coordination overhead | Designate a single volunteer lead per activation; keep briefings short and written |

---

## Summary

This strategy gives Food Bank Aid a structured, measurable approach to community fundraising that is achievable within current resources. The emphasis is on quality relationships over volume — 15 well-stewarded partnerships are more valuable than 40 poorly managed ones.

The KPIs are designed to give the team monthly visibility into progress before year-end income figures are known. The 6-month decision framework gives trustees a clear, evidence-based basis to decide whether to invest further in this strand.
